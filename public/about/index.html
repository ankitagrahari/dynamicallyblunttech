About Me

January 1, 1·3 mins

Hi, I’m Ankit Agrahari. #

Backend Development Lead | Java & Spring Enthusiast | Exploring AI Engineering

I’m a backend software engineer and technology enthusiast with 12+ years of experience building and working with backend applications and enterprise systems.

My professional journey has primarily revolved around Java, Spring, microservices and distributed systems. Over the years, I’ve worked across domains including Insurance, Channel Data Management and Automation, gaining experience in building systems, solving production challenges and helping engineering teams deliver reliable software.

I enjoy understanding how things work beneath the surface — whether it’s the internals of the JVM, Kafka’s distributed architecture, database indexing, or how modern AI systems retrieve and process information.

This blog is where I document that journey.

What I work with #

My primary areas of interest and technical experience include:

  • Backend Development: Java, Spring, Spring Boot, REST APIs, Hibernate
  • Distributed Systems: Microservices, Apache Kafka, API design, system design
  • Databases: PostgreSQL, MySQL, Redis, MongoDB, PGVector
  • Cloud & DevOps: Docker, Kubernetes, CI/CD, Prometheus
  • AI Engineering: Spring AI, RAG, Vector Databases, Embeddings, Agentic AI
  • Development Practices: Performance optimization, production troubleshooting, clean architecture and code quality

I’m particularly interested in the intersection of traditional backend engineering and AI-powered applications.

What you’ll find on this blog #

Dynamically Blunt Tech is my space for sharing practical engineering knowledge, technical explorations and lessons learned along the way.

Some of the topics I regularly explore:

  • Java and Spring Boot internals
  • Microservices and distributed systems
  • Apache Kafka and event-driven architecture
  • System design and backend engineering
  • Performance optimization and production troubleshooting
  • Generative AI, RAG and Agentic AI with Java

I prefer writing about things I’ve explored, implemented, debugged or learned rather than simply repeating documentation.
